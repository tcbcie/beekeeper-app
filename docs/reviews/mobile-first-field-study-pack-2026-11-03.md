# HiveCraic mobile field-study pack

**Prepared:** 3 November 2026  
**Status:** Session materials only. No participants recruited, sessions conducted or findings collected.  
**Purpose:** Formative evaluation with 6–8 beekeepers aged 50+, covering task use, recovery, save-state understanding and visual appeal indoors and outdoors.

## 1. Scope and evidence boundaries

Read alongside the [current mobile review](mobile-first-50-plus-review-2026-11-03.md) and [earlier Phase 5 pack](../features/mobile-first-phase5-validation-pack.md). The original review is a sampled source review. Its rendered-screen addendum covers public access screens only; neither establishes participant outcomes. This pack supplies a method, not new evidence.

The earlier pack's statements that local saving does not exist and every offline inspection fails are superseded. The current review identifies local drafts and queuing for **new inspections**. Existing-inspection edits must be tested separately. Automatic outbox attempts depend on app events; a network indicator is not evidence of server receipt. Earlier assumptions about inevitable Back-related loss also require device-specific verification. Do not teach any of these assumptions to participants before observing their understanding.

Questions to investigate:

- Can people find the right hive and complete an inspection without help?
- Can they recover work and understand the consequence of leaving or starting again?
- Can they distinguish a draft, a queued new inspection and a server-confirmed record, including photographs?
- What do they expect when editing an existing record offline or retrying a failed send?
- Are secondary forms and archive/delete choices understandable and comfortable to use?
- Which aspects of appearance support or obstruct clarity, comfort and confidence?

This small purposive sample cannot establish population prevalence, accessibility conformance or a ranking of designs. Technical checks and participant evidence remain separate.

## 2. Recruitment matrix and screening

Recruit six core participants, with two optional additions to fill coverage gaps. These are coverage targets, not assumptions about age or ability. Let people describe their own requirements; no diagnosis or medical history is needed. Participants can meet several targets. Do not exclude someone for needing assistance or recruit only confident existing users.

| Slot | Age coverage | Phone coverage | App/beekeeping experience target | Ability and preference coverage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P01 | 50–59 | Own iPhone | New to HiveCraic; any beekeeping tenure | Usual settings; self-described confidence |
| P02 | 50–59 | Own Android | Regular HiveCraic user; any tenure | Seek everyday enlarged-text use across the sample |
| P03 | 60–69 | Own Android, preferably modest handset | New to HiveCraic; newer beekeeper if available | Seek someone reporting lower phone confidence |
| P04 | 60–69 | Own iPhone | Regular HiveCraic user; experienced beekeeper if available | Seek a different handling or dexterity preference |
| P05 | 70+ | Either platform, balance totals | Occasional or new user; any tenure | Participant's normal reading/contrast settings |
| P06 | 70+ | Other platform where practicable | Any use frequency; contrast colony scale | Seek another lower-confidence participant |
| P07, optional | Fill age gap | Fill device/browser/PWA gap | Fill experience gap | Everyday VoiceOver/TalkBack or other access method, if available |
| P08, optional | Fill age gap | Fill remaining gap | Fill colony-scale gap | Fill unmet access or outdoor-use coverage |

Targets across the group: at least two users of each phone platform, two new and two regular HiveCraic users, two people describing low phone confidence, and varied colony numbers and recording habits. Seek enlarged text, varied near-vision needs and varied dexterity/access methods without assigning them to an age band. Record gaps honestly. Do not ask non-users to imitate a disability; a moderator's screen-reader check is not participant evidence.

Suggested screening questions, asked privately:

1. Which age band would you choose: 50–59, 60–69, 70+, or prefer not to say? If declining the band, can you confirm you are 50 or over?
2. What phone and browser do you normally use? Do you use installed web apps?
3. How comfortable do you feel using unfamiliar phone apps: low, medium or high, in your own terms?
4. Have you used HiveCraic? How often? Roughly how long have you kept bees and how many colonies do you manage?
5. When and where do you usually record inspections?
6. What settings, aids, positioning, breaks or assistance help you use a phone comfortably? Sharing these is optional.
7. Would a short outdoor segment in a safe seated or standing area suit you? An indoor-only session is equally welcome.

Keep contact and scheduling details separate from research records. Do not include names, precise ages, apiary addresses or health details in the analysis return. This document does not authorise recruitment messages.

Use an isolated test account and disposable fictional records. Never use a participant's production account. The session owner prepares fixtures through an authorised test setup before sessions; this pack performs no database operations.

| Fixture | Required content and purpose |

| Test apiaries | Orchard Study and Meadow Study, with enough fictional hives to require finding one |
| Target hives | Orchard 07 and Orchard 17, clearly distinct; Meadow 07 as a similar-name distractor |
| New inspection | Today's fictional observation: queen seen, six brood frames, calm; a supplied non-identifying photograph if supported |
| Existing inspection | Dated prior record with five brood frames and note “Study baseline”; retain its initial value as ground truth |
| Recovery draft | Disposable new inspection with unique note “Study recovery”; prepare only after verifying actual recovery behaviour |
| Outbox case | Disposable new inspection with a unique task code and a reproducible failed-send state |
| Secondary forms | Fictional purchase “Study feeder” and expense “Study feed”, £12.50 or the configured account currency recorded explicitly |
| Archive/delete | Study Retired hive with history; separate Study Mistake disposable hive with no valuable history |
| Information order (WP11, optional) | At Orchard Study: Orchard 03 with a Low stores reading this season, two due tasks, a visit 6 days ago; at least one monthly guidance entry and one Upcoming Event. Matches the fictional data in the WP11 mock-up |

Reset fixtures between participants and task branches; preserve evidence before resetting. Use unique participant/task codes to distinguish duplicates. Do not prepopulate real personal or apiary data. Verify that changes cannot trigger real notifications or external transactions.

Conduct a moderator-only rehearsal and record app version/commit, fixture readiness and observed states. Do not count this as a participant session. Confirm the following on each intended device/mode without assuming parity:

| State or operation | Working hypothesis from review | Rehearsal evidence to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New-inspection draft | Local recovery exists; storage/photo failures may be unclear | Exact trigger, saved content, photo presence, recovery route |
| New inspection saved offline | Can queue locally | UI wording, local entry, later server receipt |
| Existing-inspection edit offline | Does not share new-inspection draft fallback | Error/protection, retained edit, original server value |
| Failed send and retry | Manual retry exists; automatic attempts are event-driven | Failure method, retry trigger, resulting record count |
| Exit/reopen | Behaviour depends on route and platform | App exit, browser Back, closure tested separately |

Use a controlled test-network interruption for failed sends. If a state cannot be reproduced safely without app changes, mark that task blocked. A labelled screenshot scenario may test wording understanding, but must be recorded as hypothetical and excluded from live task success counts. Never assert that a connection icon proves a successful write. Verify server receipt through an authorised online view in a separate test session/device; if unavailable, record it as unverified.

**Outdoor safety:** Use a sheltered seating area or clear path well away from active hive handling, flight lines, traffic and other hazards. No live inspection, bee handling, walking while using the screen, or compulsory standing/one-handed posture. Offer seating and shade; stop or move indoors for discomfort, unsafe weather, glare, cold or fatigue. No deliberate wet-device testing or forced direct-sun exposure. Clean gloves are optional only for people who normally use them and find them comfortable; record touchscreen compatibility. Do not change a person's usual access aids to create difficulty.

Aim for paired indoor/outdoor observations for everyone who opts in; seek at least four safe outdoor segments, without making attendance conditional. Record actual conditions rather than calling overcast light “bright sunlight”. Alternate indoor-first/outdoor-first between consenting participants where practical; core inspection learning is not counterbalanced, so acknowledge it. Repeat a short hive-finding and reading task with equivalent fictional targets, not the entire task battery. Retain indoor-only participants and report their coverage separately.

## 6. Moderator script and timing

Budget approximately 75 minutes: welcome/consent 8; device/context 5; core tasks 20; offline/recovery 15; secondary/destructive-choice tasks 10; outdoor comparison 8; visual ratings/debrief 9. The optional WP11 information order block (section 7a) adds about 10 minutes; run it in a second appointment if the first is full. Offer a break around the midpoint and whenever requested. Split into two appointments if needed; do not rush to complete the battery.

**Opening:** “Thank you for helping. There are no right answers about what you like. If something is hard to use, that is useful for us to learn. Please work as you normally would and, when comfortable, say what you are looking for or expecting. I may stay quiet while you try. You can stop or skip anything.”

**Before each task:** Read only the participant prompt below. Confirm fictional facts if asked, without naming controls or routes. Start timing after the prompt is understood; stop at the participant's completion claim or decision to stop. Record interruptions separately. Do not announce a speed target.

**Neutral probes:** “What are you looking for?” “What do you expect to happen?” “What tells you that?” “Where do you think that information is now?” Avoid “Did you notice the outbox?” or “Was that easy?”

If the participant asks for help, first ask what they would try. Allow quiet thinking. After roughly 60 seconds without progress, offer to continue, receive a hint or skip; never enforce a stressful countdown. Record the exact prompt and time. A route/control hint makes completion assisted. Ordinary clarification of the fictional scenario does not. If taking over is necessary, mark not completed before demonstrating. Do not coach save-state distinctions until the participant's initial answer has been captured.

## 8. Outcomes, ratings and observation sheet

Use these mutually exclusive task outcomes:

- **U — completed unaided:** correct task result without a route/control hint.
- **A — completed with assistance:** correct result following a hint or intervention; record what was needed.
- **N — not completed:** attempted but incorrect, abandoned or moderator took over.
- **B — blocked:** fixture, connection control or technical setup prevented a meaningful attempt.
- **S — skipped:** participant declined, fatigue/time prevented attempt, or task was inapplicable; state why.

Keep task completion separate from save-state understanding and data integrity. For each applicable save task code initial understanding **correct / partly correct / incorrect / unclear**, with the participant's words and independently observed state. Record data loss **observed / none observed / unverified**, specifying fields/photos and the before/after evidence. An honest offline error can be well understood even when the requested edit cannot complete.

After each attempted task ask:

1. “How easy or difficult was that?” **1 very difficult, 2 difficult, 3 neither, 4 easy, 5 very easy**, or decline.
2. “How confident are you that the result is what you intended?” **1 not at all to 5 completely**, or decline.

High confidence with an incorrect save belief is a finding to investigate, not success.

## 9. Visual appeal and outdoor comparison

Show the actual dashboard, hive list, inspection step and recovery/error screen reached during the session. Label any prepared image as a screenshot, not an interactive result. Record screen/version, theme, text setting and environment for every rating. If a candidate exists, use matched content and viewport; alternate A/B order across participants and initially hide version labels. If no candidate exists, rate only the baseline. Do not invent a comparison.

Ask “What stands out first?”, “What feels comfortable or uncomfortable to look at?” and “What would you change, if anything?” Then collect each dimension separately:

| Dimension | 1 | 3 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Visual appeal | Very unappealing | Neither appealing nor unappealing | Very appealing |
| Reading comfort | Very uncomfortable | Neither comfortable nor uncomfortable | Very comfortable |
| Action clarity | Very unclear what to do next | Neither clear nor unclear | Very clear what to do next |

Stop all recorders, restore agreed device/network settings and sign out of the test account. Clear only study data using the agreed test reset method; never clear all personal browsing data. Give the participant their code, contact and withdrawal deadline. Record completion of cleanup without retaining device identifiers.

Store coded notes in an access-controlled research location. Keep the identity key and consent records separately with access limited to the organiser. Screen/audio recordings are identifiable source material, not anonymised returns. Review quotes, screenshots and device/profile combinations for indirect identification; generalise or suppress rare combinations. Keep any missing data as missing. ## 12. Analysis and decision method

1. Check consent/withdrawals and remove excluded material before analysis. Validate task codes, denominators, missing reasons and evidence references. Keep rehearsal and hypothetical screenshot responses out of live task totals.
2. For each task and version report raw counts: **U / A / N / B / S**, plus the number assigned and the number meaningfully attempted (**U+A+N**). Report unaided completion as **U out of attempted**, alongside blocked/skipped counts. Do not pool repeated attempts to inflate the denominator. Present first attempts separately from repeats, outdoor tasks and post-coaching attempts.
3. For T4–T7 report initial correct/partly correct/incorrect/unclear save understanding and unknown technical states. Cross-check against verified storage/receipt evidence. Report the number of unique affected participants as well as incidents; repeated taps by one person are not multiple people.
4. Group observed barriers by navigation, reading/targets, recovery, state understanding, forms and visual appearance. Keep observation, quote and interpretation distinct. If a second analyst is available later, independently code a subset and resolve disagreements; otherwise declare single-analyst interpretation. Never infer ability from age.
5. Report rating distributions as counts at each score, optionally medians with respondent counts. Keep appeal, reading comfort and action clarity separate. For paired settings/versions report individual direction of change and raw preference counts; include ties, declines and unmatched responses. Avoid significance claims or population generalisation.
6. Prioritise observed lost work, unrecoverable access barriers and false server-save beliefs even if seen once. Then assess recurring assistance, confusion and visual friction by impact and exposure. A visually preferred screen does not offset a data-integrity failure. Mark technical uncertainty for reproduction rather than calling it a confirmed cause.
7. Link each proposed change to a finding and a bounded retest. Document contrary evidence and unmet device/access/weather coverage. Publish no invented quotes, timings, ratings or success claims.

The current review proposes at least 90% unaided completion of core tasks. For a task attempted by only 6–8 people, one failure falls below that threshold, so present **6/6, 7/7 or 8/8** as the corresponding all-success counts if actually observed; these are arithmetic examples, not results. Assess each core task (T1, T2 and T4a) separately and investigate every failure. Do not apply an arbitrary 15-second pass/fail criterion to differing access methods; report start times descriptively.

Readiness discussion should also require no observed accidental loss in the tested scenarios, correct initial save-state understanding by every exposed participant, and explicit resolution of failures or a recorded decision to defer. A corrected answer after coaching is useful learning evidence but does not pass initial comprehension. A small successful study still leaves technical accessibility, untested storage failures, device gaps and real-world reliability to be verified. The study owner records the shipping decision separately; this pack grants no readiness certification.
