Proposal: The Dashboard as a “Today” Screen

Status: Phase 1 implemented (with seasonal reminders), awaiting your test  ·  Date: 29 October 2026

Your instruction: the dashboard now looks naked; the “Today around your apiaries” card adds no value and the apiary cards feel like something is missing. It is the first screen users see. Take a fresh approach and suggest what the user should actually see.

1. Start from the beekeeper, not the data

When a beekeeper opens the app they want answers, not figures. Four questions, in this order:

  1. “Can I open my hives today?” — the weather question that matters. GDD and bloom lists don’t answer it; a yes/no with the best hours does.
  2. “What should I do, and where?” — one to-do list naming hives, not counts (“Hives 3, 5, 7 not opened since August”), each with a button that starts the job.
  3. “Is anything wrong?” — only exceptions: queen problems, high varroa, a scale showing a hive getting light. Silence when all is fine.
  4. “What should I be thinking about this time of year?” — October is winter preparation, not swarm control.

Why the last two versions failed: the first showed every figure, the second showed counts (“9 hives due”, “0 tasks due”, “62 days”). Counts tell you there is a problem but not what to do about it, so the screen feels empty even when it is busy. GDD, bloom chips, nectar/pollen ratings and the stats strip are interesting, but nobody makes a decision from them on the first screen.

What each part does

  1. Greeting + one-line verdict. Replaces the stats strip: the totals that matter in one sentence.
  2. “Can I open my hives today?” A yes/no with the reason and the best window, plus the next five days as coloured tiles. Rule of thumb used by beekeepers: 15°C or more, no rain, light wind, between about 10:00 and 17:00. One forecast request for the nearest apiary (hourly temperature, rain, wind from Open-Meteo, which the app already uses). Replaces the “Today around your apiaries” strip.
  3. To do — one list, grouped by apiary (nearest first), naming the hives. Red: overdue inspections, queenless; amber: no queen signal, high varroa, scale losing weight; blue: tasks due. Each line has one button that starts the job (inspection form already filled with apiary and hive; hive page; task list). Shows the top 5, “Show all” for the rest. When nothing is due: “Nothing urgent. Next planned visit: …”.
  4. My apiaries — a compact list, one row each: hives, oldest check or weekly scale change, and a status pill. Tap for the apiary page. Replaces the big cards; the detail lives on the apiary page.
  5. This time of year — 2–3 short seasonal reminders for Ireland, changing every half-month. Fixed text to start with; later it can point at the user’s own data (“Hive 26 is light”).
  6. Kept below, unchanged: quick actions (New inspection, New task), pending invitations, “Attention needed” becomes part of To do; Upcoming events and Recent activity stay further down.

Moved off the first screen

GDD, bloom chips, nectar/pollen ratings, 7-day temperature strip and the stats strip. They stay available where they belong: the apiary page (weather, forage) and Research/Tools (GDD).

4. Suggested order

  1. Phase 1: greeting, “Can I open my hives today?”, To do with hive numbers and action buttons, compact apiary list. Remove the strip and the big cards.
  2. Phase 2: “This time of year” (you check the wording), scale “getting light” and possible-swarm alerts.
