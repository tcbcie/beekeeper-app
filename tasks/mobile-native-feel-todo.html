Mobile Native Feel — Review and Plan

Status: approved 02/11/2026 for the whole plan. No app store route for now. Phases 0 and A built, not yet committed or tested on a phone.
Date: 02/11/2026  ·  Full review: docs/features/mobile-native-feel-review-plan.md

Your brief: the app feels squeezed onto a phone rather than optimised for it, and sits far behind Revolut or The Economist. Review it, highlight the framework's constraints, and propose a plan with best practices, frameworks and skills.
Verdict. You are right about the feel, but the PWA framework is not the main cause. The earlier programme made the app safe and readable. It did not change how the app is structured or how it behaves. Most of the gap can be closed without leaving the PWA, and it needs no rewrite.

Limits of this review: layout figures were measured on production in your account, using your device emulation at 400x620. Nothing was tested on a real phone, so safe areas, the keyboard and touch are read from the code only. Screenshots failed, so apart from your screenshot I reviewed measurements, not the look of each screen.

The most serious finding: an inspection saved with no signal exists only in the phone's memory. If the app closes, it is lost. In a field app this harms trust more than any missing animation, so it comes early in the plan.

Framework constraints

A PWA cannot doHow much it mattersResponse
Haptics on iOSModerateStrong visual press states; vibration on Android
Background Sync on iOSHigh for offline savingSend on open and on reconnect; show what is waiting
Guaranteed storage on iOSModerateLocal data is a buffer, never the only long-term copy
Install prompt on iOS, or store presenceModerate for older usersGuided install screen; decide on stores later
NFC or Bluetooth on iOSLow todayAvoid depending on them

Web push does work on iOS for installed apps since 16.4, so CLAUDE.md is out of date. Screen transitions, safe areas, IndexedDB and badging are all available and unused.

Revolut is native. The Economist appears to be React Native plus native code, though that evidence is weak. A web app won't match either on haptics or gestures, but response, continuity, clear states and focused screens are all achievable.

Best practice: the mobile design contract

  • One title bar, one job and one primary action per field screen.
  • Primary action pinned in thumb reach, 56 to 64px (to be tested in gloves).
  • Forms and tall overlays become bottom sheets or full screens, never centred dialogs.
  • Rows or compact cards, no cards in cards, no sideways tables on field screens.
  • Visible press feedback within 100ms. Motion of 150 to 250ms that explains position.
  • Returning to a screen never shows a spinner if data is cached.
  • Always clear whether work is saved, waiting to send, or failed.
  • Unchanged: type floors, pinch zoom, AAA contrast, and gestures as shortcuts only. We copy the behaviour of these apps, not their small dense type.

Plan

Each phase ships alone. Scope is the shell plus Hives, Hive detail, Inspection, Records and Tasks. Admin, CRM, research, reports and desktop stay as they are. About 3 to 5 months part-time; this is a rough estimate.

Phase 0 — Baseline (days)

  • ☐ Capture the five field screens on a real iPhone and Android phone, for before and after comparison. These checks were not possible in emulation: safe areas, keyboard and touch.
  • ☑ Agree the design contract.
  • ☑ Correct the ui skill and the iOS push line in CLAUDE.md; add the mobile-screen skill.

Phase A — Feel (1 to 2 weeks)

  • ☑ Press feedback on every control through one global touch rule. Tap highlight, double-tap delay and label selection removed.
  • ☑ Safe areas with viewport-fit=cover, dvh in place of vh, and no sideways overscroll. Vertical overscroll stays until Phase E, as Android's pull to refresh is the only refresh gesture.
  • ☑ Manifest colours, a 180px Apple icon and iOS launch images.
  • ☐ Manifest screenshots. These need your real-phone captures from Phase 0.
  • ☑ System font stack. Changed from the plan: your batch 47 decision on device fonts stands, so nothing is downloaded.
  • ☑ Skeletons on Hives, Hive detail, Apiaries, Apiary detail and Tasks, plus instant feedback on every dashboard navigation. Records already had one.
  • ☑ Number keyboards and Next keys on the inspection form's number fields (inputMode, enterKeyHint).
  • ☐ Sticky hover colours on .fj-btn after a tap: moved to Phase D with the button work.

Phase B — Don't lose work (1 to 2 weeks)

  • ☑ Autosaved inspection drafts in IndexedDB, including photos, keyed by user. New inspections only; editing a saved one keeps no draft.
  • ☑ Cross-user risk answered: the owner is fixed when the form opens, and no drafts are kept during impersonation. Cleared on deliberate log-out (with a warning), password reset and account deletion. Changed from the plan: not on every sign-out, as an expired session in a field must not lose work.
  • ☑ Save timeouts and plain-language errors. A slow weather lookup no longer holds up a save.
  • ☑ Correct the offline banner wording.
  • ☐ Real-phone check: start an inspection with a photo, kill the app, reopen, Carry on. Also check autosave stays smooth with several large photos.

Phase C — Navigation (2 to 3 weeks)

  • ☑ Shared phone title bar with screen title and consistent Back, about 57px tall instead of 73px. Log out moved to Profile on phones; desktop keeps its bar.
  • ☑ Back arrows removed on phones from Hive, Apiary, Queen and Records. Duplicate h1 titles hidden visually on the main lists. Other screens' own back links remain beside the new one, to tidy with their screens.
  • ☑ Scroll position kept per bottom-bar tab. Re-tapping a tab returns to the top. It returns to the tab's main list, not a detail screen left open there.
  • ☑ Android Back closes dialogs using the shared dialog hook. Changed from the plan: uses Chrome's CloseWatcher, not history entries, which could trap users. The 18 hand-rolled dialogs follow in Phase D. Browsers without it keep their normal Back; the iPhone has no Back button.
  • ☐ Real-phone check: Back after a QR scan, tab scroll memory, Android Back on a dialog.

Phase D — Mobile primitives and field screens (4 to 6 weeks)

  • ☑ ModalShell is a bottom sheet on phones (25 dialogs in one change), scrolling inside itself and above the keyboard. Changed from the plan: uses the existing swipe hook, not Base UI Drawer, so it closes on a swipe-down rather than following the finger.
  • ☑ New StickyActionBar and SegmentedControl. Changed from the plan: no custom picker, as phones' own pickers are familiar and more accessible; no shared ListRow until a second screen needs one.
  • ☑ Record forms: their own full screen on phones. Inspection keeps Previous, Next and Save pinned, above the keyboard. Android Back closes the form through the discard guard.
  • ☑ Hives: compact rows by default, with a remembered List / Cards switch so the full card and hive-stack diagram stay available. Edit, Archive and Delete move to a sheet.
  • ☑ Hive detail: wider, title first, New Inspection pinned, other actions in two short rows, latest three of each record type with "Show all".
  • ☑ Apiary detail: hives directly under the title, New Inspection pinned.
  • ☑ Mel moved to the top bar on phones, so it no longer covers content.
  • ☑ Records and Tasks: tighter layout, Records time periods moved into Filters on phones. Record cards are unchanged, as they are already compact. The two Tasks tables stay, as they are in the printable checklist.
  • ☑ Scale pickers, vegetation popup and hive QR moved onto the shared sheet. The Visit Checklist stays hand-rolled because of its print layout.
  • ☑ Ratchet tests for hand-rolled dialogs and tables on field screens, and for vh units everywhere.
  • ☑ Button hover colours no longer stick after a tap.
  • ☐ Other screens' hand-rolled dialogs (teams, traceability, admin) remain centred. Not field screens.
  • ☐ Real-phone check: pinned Save and sheets with the keyboard open, the List / Cards switch, swipe to close.

Phase E — Instant data (3 to 4 weeks)

  • ☑ Instant return to Dashboard, Hives, Hive, Apiary, Records and Tasks, from a small per-user cache in memory and on the device, cleared on sign-out. Changed from the plan: no TanStack Query, which would have meant rewriting careful loading code across six screens for the same result. Each screen still refreshes on every visit.
  • ☑ A failed refresh keeps the last data and says how old it is, with Try again, and retries when the signal returns. Only shown on failure, not as a permanent "updated" time.
  • ☑ Records' dropdown options are saved too, so forms can be filled in without a signal.
  • ☑ Completing a task is instant, goes back if the save fails, and offers Undo. Changed from the plan: Delete and Archive keep their confirmation rather than Undo, as they are permanent or run a cascade, and delaying them could lose the action if the app closes. Saving an inspection instantly belongs to Phase G.
  • ☐ Real-phone check: move between tabs, kill the app and reopen with no signal, then regain signal.

Phase F — Motion (1 to 2 weeks)

  • ☑ Direction on phones: a new screen slides in from the right, Back from the left, tabs fade. 180ms, and off with reduced motion. Changed from the plan: no View Transitions, so no Next and React upgrade, which would have reached production unbuilt. No "morph" from a row into its detail screen.
  • ☐ Upgrade Next and React: worth doing separately, with a build test, for its own sake rather than for this.
  • ☐ Real-phone check: smooth on an older phone, and pinned bars stay still during the slide.

Phase G — Offline outbox (3 to 4 weeks)

  • ☐ Queue inspections with client-generated ids, and an idempotent insert.
  • ☐ Clear "saved on phone, waiting to send" state, with retry and a visible failure path.
  • ☐ Upload photos and fetch weather after sync.

Phase H — Validate

  • ☐ Run the moderated sessions in gloves, outdoors. These are yours to run.
  • ☐ Write the dashboard plan from what they show. It stays gated until then.

Phase I — Stores decision

  • ☐ Settle the payments question, then decide on TWA, Capacitor or neither.
