  • The map height on a phone is my estimate of the space left under the header and above the bottom bar. It needs your eye on a real phone.

Where the result differs from the plan

  • Changing an answer is two taps: remove it, then answer again. There is no single “switch” button.
  • Notes can be added when recording a sighting on the map, not when answering a predicted hotspot (that stays one tap).
  • Water: only open sea is detected. The elevation service reports exactly 0 m over sea; lakes sit above sea level and look like flat land, so they are not caught.
  • Privacy banner and legend card are gone from the page; their content is in the Layers panel, which leaves the first screen to the map.
  • Red close button in full screen is removed. The full-screen button beside it did the same job.
  • Team apiaries a team-mate also shares publicly are no longer drawn a second time as an offset purple marker.

What I found during the audit and left alone

  • If your sightings fail to load, predictions run without them and nothing says so.
  • If one apiary gets no elevation data but others do, it silently contributes no hotspots.
  • A team member with a very large number of shared apiaries could hit a request-length limit; the Apiaries screen builds the same request the same way.

5. Batch 4 proposal

In the order I would do them. Each is separate; none depends on another unless noted.

#ItemWhat it givesSize and risk
4.1 Conservation-area privacy The owner’s id stops being readable by other signed-in users. The position stays exact, since the point of declaring one is that neighbours know where it is. Small. One database function for the map to read from, and a tighter read policy. Back up first.
4.2 Database definitions into the repo dca_confirmations, conservation_areas, wild_colonies and the offset functions exist only in the live database. A copy in the repo makes them reviewable and restorable. Small. No change to the live database; read and record only.
4.3 Elevation lookups through the server, with caching Faster predictions (a 10-apiary run is about 23 requests one after another from the phone today), apiary positions no longer sent straight to an outside service, and one place to swap the provider. Do this first if the Open-Meteo terms rule out the free tier. Medium. One API route with sign-in and rate limits, plus a cache.
4.4 Prevailing wind and aspect in the engine The two biggest gaps against what is known about DCAs: drones favour sheltered, open ground. Aspect comes from heights the engine already fetches. Wind needs a climate source. Medium. Changes scoring, so it needs your known DCAs to check against.
4.5 Neighbouring colony density Shared apiaries and wild colonies already on the map count as evidence of drone supply near a candidate. Small to medium. Positions are offset by up to 5 km, so it can only be a weak signal.
4.6 Community DCA sightings Beekeepers who choose to can share confirmed DCAs, shown offset like apiaries. This is the feature the screen’s name promises. Large. Consent setting, new database function, and a decision on moderation.
4.7 Land cover and lakes Woodland edges and open ground for scoring; lakes for the water check. Large. A new map-data source; worth it only after 4.4 shows the scoring holds up.
4.8 Clustering for the all-apiaries layer Keeps the Power User view fast as registered apiaries grow. Medium. Only matters at a few hundred apiaries or more.

My recommendation: 4.1 and 4.2 together next, as they are small and close the one privacy gap. Then 4.3, then 4.4 once you can name a few DCAs you know to be real.

How much it does: little, by design. Shared apiaries are shown up to 5 km from where they are, so the signal lifts every hotspot in an area with many colonies by a similar amount; it rarely changes which of one apiary’s hotspots ranks first. In such areas a hotspot just under the cut-off can now appear.

Left out: wild colonies and the all-apiaries layer (only Power Users can see them, and a prediction should not depend on who is looking), and your own apiaries that are not ticked.

  • Shared sightings do not feed the prediction, because their positions are offset.

Batch 4.7 — land cover and lakes, built on 13 November 2026

  • ☑ Lakes: a hotspot on a lake, river, reservoir or the sea is dropped and the next best takes its place. A spot you reported yourself is kept. src/lib/dca-prediction.ts
  • ☑ Woodland: a hotspot inside woodland loses 4 points; one on open ground with woodland within 300 m gains 3. The popup’s “Signals” line can say “woodland edge nearby”.
  • ☑ Source: Mapbox’s own map data, read with the map key the app already has. src/lib/land-cover.ts
  • ☑ Checked by hand against Mapbox before building: two lakes and open sea came back as water, a Wicklow forest as woodland nearby, farmland as neither.
  • ☑ Saved predictions are worked out again (cache name moved to v8). Two new tests. Docs updated. Type-check and lint pass. Audit done, nothing to fix.
  • ☐ For you: run npx vitest run tests/lib/dca-prediction.test.ts; predict DCAs for an apiary beside a lake or a forest and check no hotspot lands on the water.

Only the hotspots about to be shown are checked, not every candidate: usually 5 to 15 requests for a prediction that is not already saved on the phone. It removes and re-ranks hotspots; it does not go looking for better ground elsewhere.

Two things to know:

  • Cost. Mapbox bills these lookups per request. I do not know your plan’s free allowance; at this volume it should be small, but it is worth a look in the Mapbox dashboard after a week.
  • Privacy. The positions of the hotspots on show (within about 4 km of an apiary, not the apiary itself) go from the phone to Mapbox, which already receives the area of map being viewed.

If the lookup fails (no signal, limit reached), the prediction is shown as before, without land cover.
